# Audio

All of Iron Ring's sound is generated by `Tools/Audio/generate_audio.py` from a model of the NES (Ricoh 2A03) sound chip: two pulse channels, the triangle and the noise channel, sequenced at 60 Hz like an NES sound driver. Decision record: [ADR-0013](adr/0013-nes-style-generated-audio.md).

```sh
pip install -r Tools/Art/requirements.txt                 # numpy, scipy (shared with the art tools)
python3 Tools/Audio/generate_audio.py                     # writes Assets/Resources/Audio/
python3 Tools/Audio/generate_audio.py --preview build/a   # also the theme as a WAV, for listening
python3 Tools/Audio/generate_audio.py --check             # CI: committed files are current
```

Encoding the music needs `ffmpeg` with libvorbis; `--check` does not.

## What plays when

| Id | Played on | Sound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `light_hit` | light attack connects | short noise burst over a falling blip |
| `heavy_hit` | heavy attack connects | deep noise hit, triangle thump, falling growl |
| `guard` | attack blocked | bright metallic 12.5 % blip |
| `breakaway` | Breakaway shove | rising double sweep over a noise whoosh |
| `tech` | throw broken | two quick high clanks |
| `slam` | throw lands | heavy floor impact with a small bounce |
| `round_call` | "ROUND n" banner | three-note call (D–A–D) |
| `round_bell` | "FIGHT!", time over, a drawn match | boxing bell, struck twice |
| `ko` | a round ends by K.O. | impact, then a long falling tone over a rumble |
| `victory` | the match is won | 3-second jingle, the theme's D minor resolving to D major |
| `menu_confirm` | (menus, M2) | two-note blip |

The fight theme, **"Iron Ring"** (`iron_ring_theme.ogg`), starts with every match and fades out when the match is decided:
- **Key and tempo:** D minor, 150 BPM.
- **Form:** a 2-bar intro (played once), then A, B and A′ (8 bars each), then a 4-bar breakdown, looping back to A.
- **Voices:**
  - pulse 1: the lead
  - pulse 2: arpeggiated chords, or an echo of the lead in B
  - triangle: driving octave bass
  - noise: drums

`music.json` records the loop point, the level and a hash of the rendered PCM.

## Formats and levels

- Effects: WAV, 44.1 kHz, mono, 16-bit, decoded once at load. Music: Ogg Vorbis q5, streamed.
- All files render at the same gain (`GAIN` in the generator), so their levels stay relative; the generator refuses to clip. Music plays at `gain` 0.5 from `music.json`.
- Mix groups: `boot.config` → `music-volume`, `sfx-volume` (0–1), or `-music-volume` / `-sfx-volume` on the command line. Effects use a pool of eight tracks; when all are busy the oldest is cut.
- The final mix passes a soft limiter (knee at −2 dBFS), so stacked impacts over the music never clip.

## Changing sounds

- An effect is a function in `Tools/Audio/chiptune/sfx.py` that writes per-frame register values (period, duty, 4-bit volume) for the channels it uses; add it to `EFFECTS`. New ids the game plays also go in `ProjectSettings/GameSettings.json → sounds` and `content::kSounds`, which AssetPacker checks.
- The theme is `Tools/Audio/chiptune/theme.py`: tracker-style lines such as `"d5:4 a4:2 r:2"` (note:rows, 6 frames per row) and per-frame instruments (volume envelope, duty sequence, arpeggio, vibrato). `bars()` rejects a bar that isn't 16 rows long.
- `chiptune/apu.py` is the chip model: period registers as on hardware, the 2A03 non-linear mixer, 4× oversampling, and Famicom-style output filters.
